# The MeleeRumble top 20, field by field: a suite (label | bench options), run from hadur-bench/:
#   mvn -q compile exec:java -Dexec.args="--suite melee-top20.txt --seeds 5 --rounds 35 --report ../docs/bench/local/<name>.md"
# or end to end with bench-melee-top20.sh / bench-melee-top20.ps1. --seeds and --rounds must come
# from the command line: a suite line's own options override the command line's, so these lines
# name only the set, --melee and the MeleeRumble's field.
#
# Why five fields. A MeleeRumble battle holds about ten robots (Hadur and nine others), so the
# 20 opponents of melee-top20-set.txt (ranks 1-3, 5-17, 19-22 of the 2026-10-05 MeleeRumble;
# Hadur is 18 and jd.Nullstride 4 has no archive jar) cannot share one battle. Twenty robots
# in at least two fields each is 40 slots; four fields of nine hold 36, five hold 45. So five
# fields of nine, every opponent in two or more of them, 45 slots in all.
#
# How they were paired. The opponents split into a strong stratum (ranks 1-10, nine robots) and a
# lower stratum (ranks 11-22, eleven robots), the way a real MeleeRumble draw mixes strong and
# weak. Every field takes four strong and five lower robots. The 5 spare slots go to the robots
# that matter most for reading Hadur: ScalarR and Neuromancer (ranks 1 and 2, the strongest) and
# Figment, Lambda and Wallaby (ranks 17, 19, 20, Hadur's immediate neighbours at 18), each of
# which stands in three fields. The fields were drawn by a seeded random search that keeps those
# counts and minimises how many robots any two fields share (at most 3 of 9 in common), so no
# two fields repeat a matchup and a robot's result is read against different company each time.
#
#   field A: ranks 1, 2, 7, 9 | 11, 14, 16, 20, 21
#   field B: ranks 2, 6, 8, 10 | 13, 17, 20, 21, 22
#   field C: ranks 3, 5, 8, 9 | 15, 16, 17, 19, 20
#   field D: ranks 1, 3, 7, 10 | 12, 14, 17, 19, 22
#   field E: ranks 1, 2, 5, 6 | 11, 12, 13, 15, 19
#
# Fields per robot: ranks 1 (A, D, E), 2 (A, B, E), 17 (B, C, D), 19 (C, D, E), 20 (A, B, C), and
# two fields for every other one. Per-opponent numbers are pooled across the fields the opponent
# stood in by data/tools/melee_pairwise.py. The suite runs the fields one after another, each in
# its own Robocode home under the suite's work directory; melee mode has no --parallel, so the
# script can also start the fields as separate processes (see bench-melee-top20.sh --fields-parallel).
A | --set melee-top20-a.txt --melee true --field 1000x1000
B | --set melee-top20-b.txt --melee true --field 1000x1000
C | --set melee-top20-c.txt --melee true --field 1000x1000
D | --set melee-top20-d.txt --melee true --field 1000x1000
E | --set melee-top20-e.txt --melee true --field 1000x1000
