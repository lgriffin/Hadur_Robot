# Examples first

Before the property, write a handful of plain examples in `EnergyLedgerTest`, tagged `HL-14` or
`HL-15`. Each pins down one row of the table in step 1, and each name says what it proves.

| Test | Proves |
|------|--------|
| the first scan has nothing to compare with | never a shot |
| a plain drop is a shot of that power | 100 to 98.5 is a shot of 1.5 |
| a drop that is exactly our bullet's damage is not a shot | `HL-16` at unit level |
| a shot hidden inside a bigger drop is found once our hit is taken out | two causes at once |
| when its bullet hits us the enemy gains 3 times the power, and its shot is still found | the refund |
| stopping dead from full speed is a wall hit and costs speed / 2 - 1 | the wall |
| a shot in the same interval as a wall hit is still found | wall plus shot |
| braking normally is not a wall hit | the inference's other side |
| a corrected drop outside 0.1 to 3.0 is not a shot | `HL-15` |
| the limits themselves, 0.1 and 3.0, are shots | the tolerance |
| hits are forgotten once the interval closes | one hit explains one drop |

The shape of one of them:

```java
EnergyLedger ledger = new EnergyLedger();
ledger.scan(100, 0);
ledger.ourBulletHit(0.5);
EnergyLedger.Reading r = ledger.scan(98, 0);
assertFalse(r.shot());
```

Our bullet of power 0.5 does `4 * 0.5 = 2.0` damage. The raw drop is 2.0, the corrected drop is 0,
and no shot is reported.

Write these before the ledger body is complete. Run them and watch them go red, then green as you
fill in each line of `scan`. The examples are the specification. The property in the next step
checks that you did not miss a combination.
