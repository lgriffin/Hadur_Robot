# Release notes

A release note is the last piece of evidence. It tells someone who was not there what changed, why, and how sure you are.

## Where it goes

`docs/releases/` in Hadur has one file per version (`v3.5.md` is the one to read). Add `docs/releases/v-lab16.md` to your lab. Leave the robot's version at 0.1 unless you also change it in the parent pom, in `Hadurling.properties` (`robot.version`) and in the bench's `--robot-jar` and `--robot` options; the jar's name comes from the pom, and the bench looks for `hadurling.Hadurling 0.1` by default.

## The shape

Hadur's notes follow this shape. Copy it.

1. **Opening line.** One sentence: what this release is, and the evidence that started it (an issue, a bench result).
2. **Download.** Where the jar is and what Robocode calls it. For your lab: the file name of `hadurling-robot`'s jar.
3. **Why.** The problem, with a number. Hadur 3.5: 3.4 was 20th, but scored 1.7 points under expectation against the 716 robots ranked below 500, about 1.0 APS, mostly by dropping rounds to rammers and mirror movers.
4. **What changed.** One bullet per requirement ID, in a sentence a player could follow.
5. **Bench.** A before and after table, with the opponents, the number of seeds and battles, and the interval or verdict.
6. **Limits and what did not work.** The nanos that shoot well at mid range gained little. Say what your feature does not help.

## What to include

- The requirement IDs, so the notes link to the rows and the rows link to the tests.
- The verdict from step 6, in its exact form (gain, neutral, harm), with the numbers.
- The conditions: cold or warm, seeds, rounds, opponents.
- What you re-recorded or re-pinned on purpose (fixtures, snapshots).
- Known limits, and any open question. If a trigger has only been checked against five bots, say so.

## What to leave out

- Words like "improved" without a number
- A table that omits the opponents it got worse against
- A claim the bench does not support. If the interval spans 0, the sentence is "no measurable change".

## A worked example

From Hadur 3.5 (`docs/releases/v3.5.md`):

> **MIR-1:** against a robot that drives to the reflection of our position through the field's centre, Hadur drives a path planned 110 ticks ahead and aims at the reflection of that path. 3.4's gun hit such robots about 8% of the time.
>
> Both stay off against the top 19: the gates are behaviour no top-19 robot showed in 81 bench battles.

Read what it does. It says the behaviour, gives the old number, and states how the feature is kept off where it could do harm, with the count of battles behind the claim.

## Write yours

Keep it under a page. Then ask someone, or imagine someone, who has not seen the lab: could they decide whether to upgrade from this note alone?

## Check yourself

- Does the first sentence say what and why?
- Is every number in the note in your bench report, with the same value?
- Does it name at least one thing the feature does not do?
