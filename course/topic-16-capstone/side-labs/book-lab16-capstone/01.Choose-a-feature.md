# Choose a feature

Pick one of four. Each is a feature Hadur actually shipped, built here in a small form. The Hadur version is deliberately bigger and has been tuned against strong robots, so you will compare with it at the end and not copy it.

Choose by what you want to practise.

| Option | You practise | Hadur's version |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Run from a rammer | a detector that must not fire on strong robots, and a small search over headings | RAM-2 |
| 2. Bullet-shadow scoring | geometry checked against a brute-force twin | MOVE-1 |
| 3. A mirror-mover detector | a detector plus a prediction that uses your own plan | MIR-1 |
| 4. A two-posture gate | failing closed, and keeping one behaviour untouched | GATE-1 to GATE-5 |

## Option 1: run from a rammer

A rammer drives straight at you and fires at point-blank range. A surfer cannot dodge those shots, and an orbit holds a distance but does not run. Hadur 3.4 spent most of each round within 100 px of a rammer.

Hadur's requirement, from `docs/requirements.md`:

> RAM-2 (State): Once charges (the enemy's own speed toward us 6 px/tick or more for four scans running) have come within 120 px, with both robots above 20 energy, in two different rounds, for the rest of the battle, while the enemy has closed for two scans running within 500 px and until it is more than 650 px away, movement shall drive the heading that keeps a pursuing enemy furthest away over the next 20 ticks, and the core shall fire as RAM-1 does.

Hadurling's version: a `RammerDetector` in `policy` (pure values in, a yes or no out) and a `RamEscape` in `move` that picks, among 24 headings, the one that keeps a simple pursuing model of the enemy furthest away over 20 ticks.

Bench: Hadurling against `sample.RamFire`, plus the other sample bots as a regression set. The risk to watch is the detector firing on bots that only come close for a moment.

## Option 2: bullet-shadow scoring

An enemy bullet and one of yours that cross in flight destroy each other. The angles where that happens are safe angles for you.

Hadur's requirement:

> MOVE-1 (Ubiquitous): Movement shall exclude bullet-shadowed guess-factor intervals from a wave's danger score.

Hadurling's version: a pure function from an enemy `Wave` and your bullets in flight to a list of shadowed bearing intervals, and a `Surfer` that scales the danger at a shadowed angle down.

Test: a jqwik property that flies an enemy bullet at many angles and your bullet turn by turn, and requires the angles that collide to be exactly the shadows. This is the model-based property you wrote for the k-d tree in lab 08.

Bench: the honest expectation is a small effect against the sample bots, which rarely hit a moving target. A verdict of "no measurable change" is a good outcome, and step 6 shows how to say it.

## Option 3: a mirror-mover detector

Some simple robots (stelo.MirrorNano, stelo.MirrorMicro) drive to the reflection of your position through the centre of the field. Their path is exactly predictable from yours.

Hadur's requirement:

> MIR-1 (State): While the enemy has stayed within 30 px (running average) of the reflection through the field's centre of our own position of up to 8 ticks before for 120 scans running with both robots above 10 energy (30 once confirmed this battle), and until that error exceeds 80 px, movement shall follow a path planned at least 110 ticks ahead and the gun shall aim at the reflection of where that path has us when the bullet arrives.

Hadurling's version: a `MirrorDetector` that keeps a running error for each lag of 0 to 8 ticks, and an aim that fires at the reflection of where your own planned path puts you when the bullet arrives. Start with the detector and a fixed plan; planning ahead is the stretch goal.

Bench: you need a mirror opponent. Write a tiny sparring robot that drives to the reflection of your position, build its jar, and give it to the bench with `--opponent-jar` (then name it with `--opponent`).

## Option 4: a two-posture gate

Hadurling has only a duel brain. Add a second brain for several opponents, and a gate that chooses.

Hadur's requirements:

> GATE-1 (State): While two or more opponents are alive, no sentry robot is alive, no sentry has been scanned this round and the melee subsystems have not failed this round, the core shall drive the robot with the melee subsystems instead of the duel subsystems.
>
> GATE-2 (Unwanted): If fewer than two opponents are alive, then the core shall drive the robot with the duel subsystems from that same tick.
>
> GATE-3 (Unwanted): If a scanned robot reports that it is a sentry, then the core shall use the duel subsystems for the rest of the round and shall treat the sentry border as a wall.
>
> GATE-4 (Unwanted): If the melee subsystems throw, then the core shall record the fault and use the duel subsystems for the rest of the round.
>
> GATE-5 (Ubiquitous): The core shall never target, count or profile a sentry robot as an opponent.

Hadurling's version: a `PostureGate` with a `Posture` enum and a veto, a deliberately simple melee brain (move away from the nearest opponent, fire at it), and the faults-fall-back-to-duel behaviour. Treat sentries as optional: do GATE-1, GATE-2 and GATE-4 first.

Bench: the duel must not change. Run the existing duel bench before and after and require the paired difference to include 0. A melee battle is a bonus.

## Decide

Write your choice at the top of `docs/requirements.md` as a one-line comment, or in a new `docs/capstone.md`. Then answer in two sentences:

- What does Hadurling do badly today that this feature should fix?
- What would the bench show if it worked, and what would it show if it did not?

If you cannot answer the second question, choose again. A feature you cannot measure is not a stage.
