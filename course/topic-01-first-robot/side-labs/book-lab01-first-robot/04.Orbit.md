# Orbit the enemy

A robot that stands still is easy to hit. Circling the enemy, always at a right angle to it,
keeps you moving across its aim. It is the simplest movement that works.

## Side-on

The bearing is 0 when the enemy is straight ahead, and positive when it is to our right. If we
turn so the enemy is at a bearing of 90 degrees (pi / 2), it is exactly on our right-hand side:
we are side-on to it. Driving forwards, we circle it. So the turn we need is the current
bearing plus a quarter turn, taken the short way round. Add to `onScannedRobot`:

```java
setTurnRightRadians(Utils.normalRelativeAngle(e.getBearingRadians() + Math.PI / 2));
setAhead(STEP * direction);
```

`setAhead(100)` drives 100 pixels forward. A negative number drives backwards. We queue it
again on every scan, so there is always more road ahead and the robot never stops.
`direction` is the field from step 2: 1 forwards, -1 backwards.

## Do not get stuck on a wall

The battlefield has walls. If the orbit takes the robot into one, the engine calls
`onHitWall`. Override it and turn around:

```java
@Override
public void onHitWall(HitWallEvent e) {
    direction = -direction;
}
```

Add `import robocode.HitWallEvent;`. Because `direction` is a **field**, this change is
remembered, and the next `setAhead(STEP * direction)` goes the other way. A local variable
would have been forgotten the moment the method returned. This is the other half of what
fields are for.

## Checkpoint

The robot now locks its radar and circles its enemy. Build it and watch it against
`sample.SittingDuck`: it should drive a circle round the duck and bounce off walls if the
circle is too big for the field.
