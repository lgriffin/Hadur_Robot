# The power policy

Landing a power-3 bullet deals 16 damage and refunds 9 energy to the shooter. Against a gun that
rarely hits us, full power pays whatever the distance. Against an enemy that shoots well, or when we
do not hit, it is waste. So the policy needs evidence on **both** sides.

Create `PowerPolicy`:

```java
public static boolean certain(Estimate ours, Estimate theirs) {
    if (Double.isNaN(ours.value()) || Double.isNaN(theirs.value())) return false;
    return ours.lower() > OUR_RATE && theirs.upper() < THEIR_RATE;
}

public static double power(double gunPower, Estimate ours, Estimate theirs, double ourEnergy,
        double enemyEnergy) {
    if (!(ourEnergy > MIN_OUR_ENERGY) || !certain(ours, theirs)) return gunPower;
    return Math.max(gunPower, Math.min(FULL_POWER, enemyEnergy / 4.0));
}
```

Read the `certain` line as the requirement. Our rate is certainly above 20% when the **lowest** end
of its interval is above 20%. Theirs is certainly below 10% when the **highest** end is below 10%.
The margin is built into the comparison. There is no separate "do we have enough samples" check
that could disagree with it: thin evidence has a wide margin, so it fails the comparison by
itself.

Everything else keeps the gun's own power. The last line caps full power at a quarter of the
enemy's energy: more than that is wasted on a nearly dead target. And it never goes *below* what the
gun asked for.

## Test the line from both sides

A policy test that only checks "it fires when it should" proves nothing. Each of these tests is
tagged, and the names say what is on the other side of the line:

```java
@Test
@Tag("HL-28")
@DisplayName("one hit in one shot is a raw 100% and still not enough")
void thinEvidenceOfOurs() {
    Estimate ours = rate(1, 1);
    Estimate theirs = rate(0, 100);
    assertEquals(1.0, ours.value(), 0);
    assertFalse(PowerPolicy.certain(ours, theirs));
    assertEquals(1.0, PowerPolicy.power(1.0, ours, theirs, 100, 100), 0);
}
```

| Test | Tag | What it holds |
|------|-----|---------------|
| a full window that is certain on both sides fires full power | `HL-27` | the action |
| one hit in one shot is a raw 100% and still not enough | `HL-28` | thin evidence of ours |
| thirty shots at them with none landing is still not enough to call their gun harmless | `HL-28` | thin evidence of theirs |
| unknown rates hold the gun's own power | `HL-28` | `NONE` |
| rates on the wrong side of their lines hold back, however many samples | `HL-28` | plenty of evidence, wrong answer |
| with 12 energy or less, the gun's power-down stands | `HL-28` | low energy |
| full power is capped at a quarter of their energy | `HL-27` | the cap |

The second row is worth a second look. Thirty shots with no hit sounds like proof their gun is
harmless. The interval at 0 from 30 is still wide enough that its top end is above 10%, so the
policy keeps holding. That is the policy doing what it was told.

Notice the energy rule: with 12 energy or less, full power is out whatever the evidence says,
because a full-power miss could leave us disabled.
