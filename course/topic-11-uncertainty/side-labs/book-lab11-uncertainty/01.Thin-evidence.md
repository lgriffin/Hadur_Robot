# Thin evidence

Lab 10 saved how often each side hit. A policy will want to use those numbers: if our gun hits more
than one shot in five, and theirs hits less than one in ten, fire full power. Try it on paper.

| Our record | Raw rate | Should we believe it? |
|-----------|----------|------------------------|
| 1 hit from 1 shot | 100% | no |
| 2 hits from 5 | 40% | hardly |
| 40 hits from 100 | 40% | yes |

The raw rate of the second and third rows is the same, and they are not the same evidence. What is
different is how many shots stand behind the number. A **confidence interval** puts that in the
number. A 95% interval for a rate says: "the true rate is probably in here". With one shot the
interval is nearly the whole range 0 to 1. With a hundred it is narrow.

There is a second problem. The enemy changes. A rate over the whole battle is the wrong thing to
trust when it changed two rounds ago. So the rate is over a **window**: the last hundred outcomes
and no more.

## The plan

1. `Estimate`: a rate and a margin, with a known formula.
2. `HitWindow`: a rolling window of recent outcomes that makes an `Estimate`.
3. `PowerPolicy`: fire full power only when we are certain, on both sides.
4. `SeedTrust`: let a remembered profile fade when the live evidence says it is wrong.

All four are plain Java with no Robocode and no clock. They can be tested with a few numbers.

The most important sentence of the lab: **when in doubt, a policy keeps doing what it did before.**
Every test below checks that the policy holds back when it should, and acts when it should.
