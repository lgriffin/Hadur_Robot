# Why a number needs an interval

Fight Hadurling against `sample.Crazy` for 10 rounds and it scores 91% of the points. Fight it again
and it scores 84%. Which is right? Both. A Robocode battle has randomness in it: where the robots
start, where they move, which bullets land. One battle is one draw from a spread of possible
outcomes.

Two ideas make results usable.

## A seed makes a battle repeatable

Robocode takes its random numbers from a seed. Give it the same seed and the same two robots and the
battle is the same, tick for tick. So the bench fights seeds 1, 2, 3, and so on. The same command
gives the same numbers, and any battle can be re-run.

Robocode reads the seed once, when its JVM starts, from the system property `RANDOMSEED`. That has
a consequence you will meet in step 5: **each battle runs in a JVM of its own.**

## Many seeds make an interval

With five seeds you have five scores. The mean of those is your estimate. The spread of those tells
you how far to trust it. A 95% confidence interval is made by a method that, repeated many times, gives an interval
containing the true mean about 19 times in 20.

```
75.1% +/- 6.0 (n=5)
```

That reads: the mean score share is 75.1%, and the true value is probably between 69% and 81%.

## What is a score share?

Robocode scores a battle for each robot. The **score share** is ours divided by both scores
together. 50% is a draw, 100% is a shutout. It is a good number to track because it does not depend
on how many rounds were fought, and it is what RoboRumble ranks by.

## Two questions, two tools

1. How good is this version? One list of shares gives a mean and an interval.
2. Is this version better than that one? Two lists, paired by seed, give an interval of
   *differences*.

The second one is where people go wrong. Section 4 explains how.

## What stays out of `mvn verify`

A battle takes seconds to minutes and is random. If it were part of the build, the build would be
slow and would fail for reasons unrelated to your change. So the line is:

- **the arithmetic** (`Stats`, `Report`, argument parsing) is a unit test, fast and exact
- **the battles** are run by hand, with a command, when you want a measurement

The rest of this lab keeps that line.
