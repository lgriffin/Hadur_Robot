# Run `mvn verify`

```sh
cd course-labs/lab-13
mvn -B verify
```

Expect `BUILD SUCCESS` in all three modules, with the traceability test passing for `HL-1` to
`HL-41`. The scale tests finish quickly, because they count and do not wait.

## The replay fixture

It is unchanged. The scripted duel carries no timing events, so the budget stays at level 0 and the
orders are the same as in lab 12. If the replay test fails, you changed play when nothing was slow:
the most likely place is the `level` argument passed to the gun.

If you want a replay that does shed work, record one: add a `TickTime` of 3 ms and a `SkippedTurn`
to a copy of `ScriptedBattle`, regenerate that copy's fixture, and check that the orders change at the
point you expect. Do not replace the main fixture for it.

## Check that the budget is worth it

Run the bench from lab 12 against a robot that has had its tick budget removed (set
`ALLOWANCE_NANOS` to `Long.MAX_VALUE` so no tick is ever slow), and against the lab as built. On a fast
machine they will agree, because nothing is slow. That is the answer you want: the budget costs nothing
when it is not needed. It earns its keep on a slow machine, which you can imitate by a small
`Thread.sleep` in the core's loop (in a scratch copy, never in the lab).

## What you finished

From the first lab to this one, Hadurling grew into a robot that:

- has a pure, replayable core behind an adapter, guarded by ArchUnit
- is specified by 41 requirements, each traced to a test
- learns where the enemy goes, and dodges what the enemy fires
- accounts for every change in the enemy's energy
- remembers opponents on disk, and survives being killed mid-save
- holds back on thin evidence, and sheds work when it runs long
- can be measured, with an interval, against its own past

The capstone starts here.
