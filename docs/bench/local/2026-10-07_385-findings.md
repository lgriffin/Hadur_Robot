# Findings: Hadur 3.8.5 overnight bench (issue #117)

Written 2026-10-07 after the overnight queue (`plans/overnight-385.queue`, run by `benchqueue.py`)
finished. This page is the summary. The detail is in the linked documents and the raw rows in
`data/bench/`. Nothing in `hadur-core` or `hadur-robot` was changed. The bench reports what was
measured and how sure we are; what Hadur does next is the owner's call.

## Answer in one paragraph

3.8.5 is level with 3.8 in 1v1, melee and team, and we can now say so with a stated width: over
100 seeds on the 1v1 top 20 the pooled difference is -0.32 points (opponent-clustered interval
-0.71 to +0.07, equivalent to 3.8 within one point). The one real, resolved loss in the whole
history is older: **3.5.1 lost 6.4 points to 3.4 on the live-loser set**, 14 opponents down after
Holm, and 3.7 and 3.8.5 have not won it back (3.8.5 against 3.4 is -6.7). Every later step is
level. So the gap to chase is a 3.4 to 3.5.1 regression, not anything in 3.7 to 3.8.5, and the
largest absolute gaps (BeepBoop 25.3%, ScalarR 32.0%) are shared by 3.8 and 3.8.5 alike.

## What the owner can act on

1. **The 3.4 to 3.5.1 change is the only resolved regression.** Fourteen opponents go down after
   Holm. If Hadur is to be evolved, start by reading what changed in that release, not later ones.
   Caveat below on selection.
2. **The big absolute gaps are not new.** BeepBoop (25.3%) and ScalarR (32.0%) are lost to by both
   3.8 and 3.8.5. DrussGT (46.6%), Diamond, Wavelet and Gilgalad sit within about two points of
   even. These are matchup problems, not 3.8.5 problems.
3. **3.8.5 does not need rolling back** on any evidence here. The cost, if any, is under 0.7
   points in 1v1. Melee duress is higher (99 ticks a battle against 29 for 3.7); the score effect
   is not resolved but it is the number to watch.
4. **More seeds on single opponents is poor value.** Planning for ±1.0 per opponent needs 138 to
   252 seeds each. Pooled and clustered figures answer the release question; per-opponent runs are
   only for a named suspect.

## How far to trust it

- **Selection bias.** The live-loser set was picked from 3.4's live results, so some of the
  3.4 advantage is regression to the mean. The leak-set check (3.8 vs 3.4, +0.66, clustered
  interval spanning zero) says the gap is not general. Read R3 as a real loss on the set that
  matters for live ranking, not as a global one.
- **Clustering matters.** Opponent-clustered intervals are 2.5 to 3.3 times wider than per-battle
  ones. Quote the clustered one when the question is about opponents in general.
- **Tight runs are load tests, not results.** R4 (+0.70) and R5 (+2.15) used CPU constant 400000;
  both builds collapse to about 30% share with 112 to 147 skipped turns per battle. They are
  floor effects and are not used.
- **Gate.** The 100-seed run is CAUTION: 328 of 2000 pairs untrusted, mostly duress. Dropping the
  duress pairs moves the pooled difference by 0.01. The bench's own trust count (1359 of 2000)
  holds a missing last-round record against a battle on both builds; the analysis gate does not
  (1810). The kaizen entry `kaizen/2026-10-07-top20big-trusted-count.md` proposes aligning them.
- **Conditions.** CPU constant 1488498, parallel 12, `--child-cpus 2`, child heap capped at 2G
  (tier2 uncapped), Threadripper PRO 9965WX, the owner's live clients running (up to 3 other
  Robocode JVMs). The earlier rumble workers were stopped during the queue after memory pressure
  without a heap cap; the cap and `Memory.java` guard (#121) came from that. `zen.Ronin` failed
  every battle in every run and is excluded. The CPU sweep was dropped at the owner's request.
- **XanderCat "security errors 100"** is one harmless engine denial per battle, seen on both
  builds. It does not invalidate those rows.
