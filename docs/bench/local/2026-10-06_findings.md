# Findings: the first local 1v1 bench

Issue #102, 6 October 2026. Hadur 3.8 against 3.7, paired by seed, cold: the top-20 set at 5
seeds x 35 rounds, and DrussGT alone at 40 seeds. Sources: `2026-10-06_top20-3.8-vs-3.7.md` (with
a folder of per-opponent files), `2026-10-06_drussgt-40-3.8-vs-3.7.md`, `2026-10-06_parallel-ladder.md`
and the two `data/bench/2026-10-06_hadur-3.8_*-local_cold.tsv` files.

## Conditions

Threadripper PRO 9965WX, 24 cores, 48 logical, Windows 11, Java 21.0.10, Robocode 1.9.5.6.
`--parallel 12`, each battle JVM told it has 2 CPUs. Six RoboRumbleAtHome clients (four 1v1,
one melee, one team) ran the whole time. The CPU constant was pinned at 1488498, read from the
rumble client's `C:\robocode\config\robocode.properties`. Candidate and baseline battles ran in
the same bench under the same load. All 200 top-20 battles and all 80 DrussGT battles finished
with 0 faults.

## Seeds for a ±3 pp interval

The 5-seed interval is 2.78 x SD / √5, so SD = interval x 0.80. Seeds needed is
(t x SD / 3)², with t about 2.0 to 2.4 at these sizes. Raven: SD 10.6, t 2.01, so
(2.01 x 10.6 / 3)² = 50.4, rounded up to 51 once t is checked. Nene's SD is 2.5, so 6 seeds.
Across all 20 it is 472 paired seeds, against 100 run. SD from 5 values is rough: DrussGT read
6.1 over 5 seeds (19 seeds) and 7.9 over 40 (about 30), so add a third.

Run first: Nene and GresSuffurd (10 seeds each), the cheap rows where 3.8 reads lower on robots
Hadur beats. Then Wavelet (23). Then Raven: 20 seeds gives ±5.0, enough to resolve a loss near
10 points, though ±3 needs 51. Then Tomcat (36) and XanderCat (43), the plan's conflicting-signal
robots, expensive because they are noisy. Then Firestarter, Gilgalad and Knight (25 each). Last:
Saguaro (47, one seed at +21.2 drives the spread) and Phoenix (46).

## What it says about 3.8

Pooled over per-battle pairs, the top-10 rows read +6.5 ± 3.2 (35 of 50 up), in line with the
cloud release check's +7.2 ± 6.3. All 20 read +2.8 ± 2.0 (55 of 100 up). The split is by 3.7's
share. On the 8 robots where 3.7 scored under 50% the mean is +9.8 ± 2.7 (34 of 40 up), and all
eight points are positive. On the 12 where it scored over 50% the mean is -1.8 ± 2.2 (21 of 60
up), which spans zero, and it holds both resolved losses. The six DrussGT-route targets read
+11.3 ± 3.3 (26 of 30 up), three resolved. I drew the 50% line after seeing the rows, so it is
a pattern to test, not a result, and the data say nothing about why.

Other points. BeepBoop survival share is 13.1% against 2.3%, and rounds won 23 against 4 of
175, the first move off zero in the record. Neuromancer skipped the most turns in both builds
(56.4 and 69.6 a battle). Local did not reproduce D1's Diamond ±1.3 (+14.1), Firestarter +21.3
or Tomcat -11.2.

## Inconsistencies in the sources

The ladder doc says the header names the CPU constant; the pinned runs' headers read "unknown".
It says 12 wide stays within about 5 skips of sequential, but 15.5 against 8.4 is 7.1. The pinned
1488498 differs from the ladder's calibrated 1876154, and its file records version.lastrun
1.11.1 against the bench's 1.9.5.6. `drussgt.txt` calls DrussGT rumble-2, `top20.txt` rumble-3.
`hadur-bench/README.md` still advises one battle per two cores.
