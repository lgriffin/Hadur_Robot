## What it says

- **The surf was hit where it chose to be.** 78% of the damage (10.2 of 13.1 a round) landed
  inside the surf's own plan for that wave, and the plan was current: 8.6 of the 10.2 came from
  a plan published the tick before the hit. Prediction fidelity (we drove somewhere other than
  where we planned) is the off-plan share, 1.0 a round, 8%. The same holds against the top 200,
  where 93% is on-plan.
- **It chose mid-range spots it rated as a real risk.** On-plan damage by distance: 0.7 under
  150 px, 1.3 at 150-300, 5.6 at 300-500, 2.6 beyond 500. The views rated the hit angle mid (6.1)
  or known (2.6) more than safe (1.6). The danger the surf gave the plans that were hit has a
  median of about 0.19 (its estimated chance of being hit there), and a tenth of that damage came
  from plans it rated above 0.38. It knowingly took a risky spot, which suggests the three
  options often had nothing much safer to offer (inferred; the options it passed over are not
  recorded).
- **The surf never changed mode.** No battle logged a `move-flavour` change, so every wave was
  surfed with the three options: orbit clockwise, orbit counter-clockwise, or stop, each at full
  commitment. Go-to surfing (MOVE-2's second flavour, which picks stop points every 2 ticks along
  both orbits) never ran. It lost S6's A/B, but that was a much weaker robot with a different
  danger model.
- **No-plan damage is mostly the rammer escape.** Of 2.0 a round taken with no plan on the
  whole 400 (shield-listed robots included), 1.2 came while RAM-2's escape drove, mostly
  head-on and linear shots inside 150 px; it concentrates in ranks 201-400 (6.8 a round there).
  The rest is head-on fire on waves the surf had not planned for (0.4) and bullets with no wave
  (0.1).

So the movement lever is the option set, not learning, detection or prediction: the danger
model sees the risk, and the three full-commitment options do not reach a safer spot often
enough.
