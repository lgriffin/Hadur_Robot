# View ranking read: which danger view orders the three options best (2026-11-09)

Zero PC hours. Data: the two field-prior forensic runs already on disk (`work/prior-forensic`, `work/prior-forensic2`;
32 learning-flat guns from `hadur-bench/prior-forensic-cloud.txt`, seeds 10600 and 10700, 2 seeds each), baseline arm `c` only,
so this is the shipped surf as it stands on master. Every AES record logs each option's weight x density and weight x scan
weight per view, so each view's own danger per option can be rebuilt and scored the way the blend is scored in
`data/tools/aes_round.py`: on waves where exactly one of the present options was hit by the real bullet, how often did the
model rank that option strictly top (chance 1/3). Scripts: scratchpad `view_rank.py`, `rank_pos.py`, `opt_geom.py`.

## Summary (2026-11-10 07:30 UTC, after eight routes)

Eight changes to the surf were run on the cloud against the same 32 learning guns and scored on the bullets that
really hit, round by round, at zero PC hours: re-weighting the danger views, committing to the last option,
half-speed options, hold-then-brake options, a prior from the opponent's own bullets, a member that models the
gun's recent aims, the refitted shared terms (hit rate, time to impact, distancing, uncertainty) and the views'
kernel width at half and double. None cut real hits: every arm sits inside +-0.004 hits per wave of the shipped
surf or above it, and the three that looked best offline (the oracle's bits, the opponent prior, the term refit)
went the wrong way for real. So the 1v1 movement gap is NOT in how the surf orders its options, NOT in the shape
or weighting of its danger model, and NOT in where its options lie on the wave: with the information it has, the
surf's choice is at a flat optimum, and an offline gain at the first rating does not predict real hits. What the
reads do not cover, and where to look next, is outside the surf's choice: how many bullets are aimed at us at
all (distance kept, how much we fire and so how much the enemy fires back, the energy trade), how much each enemy
gun learns from our movement over a battle (the real hit rate by round is flat for the shipped surf, 0.091 to
0.086, so the learning guns are not gaining on us within a battle; the comparison to make is against BeepBoop's
hit rate by round under the same guns), and the movement's execution between ratings (the hits land inside the
surf's last plan, so the bullet finds us where the surf said we would be). **Added 08:00 UTC: the trace study against BeepBoop under the same guns (last section) answers the
second of these: it is not learning. The guns hit BeepBoop 27% less per bullet from round 0, flat by round for
both robots, lower on 30 of 32 guns, and the gap is largest against the simpler guns (band 5: 0.032 vs 0.064), three
of which barely hit BeepBoop at all; the next instrument is per-gun, on those three.**

## Reading

- The blend beats every single view and the equal mix in every round bucket from round 1 on; the member terms add less than
  a point over the views alone (0.431 vs 0.424). The views range 0.37-0.42; the flatteners' round-0 numbers rest on 14 waves.
- The hit option is the one the first rating would have picked on 25-26% of single-hit waves (chance 33%): the whole danger
  model's edge at first rating is about 8 points over chance, and no view carries more than that on its own.
- The blend's margin is flat: the hit option's danger is at median 0.93 of the most dangerous other option, so a different
  weighting of the same views moves few orderings.
- Per opponent the blend only orders well against simple guns (QuantumChromodynamics 0.75, PatrollerBot 0.64); against the
  learning guns that hold the lost points it sits at 0.39-0.45, and the best single view is no better.
- 63% of waves hit none of the three options at first rating (the bullet lands outside all three ranges); 34% hit one.

## Consequence

Re-weighting the existing views is not a lever: the family as a whole, not its mix, is close to chance against learning
guns at the moment the surf first orders its options. A danger member has to bring information the views do not hold
(the field prior tried to and lost; the per-opponent prior would be the next of that kind), or the gain lies in what happens
after the first rating. Next instrument: the same scoring on the LAST rating before the bullet arrives, to see how much of
the 25% -> 10% drop in hit share the re-ratings earn and whether it is the views or the option geometry that moves.

## Where this leaves the 1v1 movement question

- Not the mix of views, not commitment. The first rating's edge over chance is 8 points; the re-ratings add 1.4 more.
- The hits the surf foresees and still takes (54% of hits in the chosen first option) are the largest class: at that
  moment every option it has is rated close, and the one it takes gets hit. More options, or options further apart
  (a wider stop, a reverse), is the untested geometry lever; the real-hit column of `aes_round.py` is its gate.
