## Reading

- The blend beats every single view and the equal mix in every round bucket from round 1 on; the member terms add less than
  a point over the views alone (0.431 vs 0.424). The views range 0.37-0.42; the flatteners' round-0 numbers rest on 14 waves.
- The hit option is the one the first rating would have picked on 25-26% of single-hit waves (chance 33%): the whole danger
  model's edge at first rating is about 8 points over chance, and no view carries more than that on its own.
- The blend's margin is flat: the hit option's danger is at median 0.93 of the most dangerous other option, so a different
  weighting of the same views moves few orderings.
- Per opponent the blend only orders well against simple guns (QuantumChromodynamics 0.75, PatrollerBot 0.64); against the
  learning guns that hold the lost points it sits at 0.39-0.45, and the best single view is no better.
- 63% of waves hit none of the three options at first rating (the bullet lands outside all three ranges); 34% hit one.

## Consequence

Re-weighting the existing views is not a lever: the family as a whole, not its mix, is close to chance against learning
guns at the moment the surf first orders its options. A danger member has to bring information the views do not hold
(the field prior tried to and lost; the per-opponent prior would be the next of that kind), or the gain lies in what happens
after the first rating. Next instrument: the same scoring on the LAST rating before the bullet arrives, to see how much of
the 25% -> 10% drop in hit share the re-ratings earn and whether it is the views or the option geometry that moves.

**MOVE-36 commitment arm (cloud only, removed).** Hypothesis: with the danger margin flat (hit option at median 0.93 of
the next), the option flips between ratings on noise, and holding the option taken last tick at 0.8 of its danger would
keep the first dodge. Run `work/commit-forensic` (same 32 guns, seeds 10801-10802, arms c = aa,aesrec and cm =

rating's own hit share also rose (+0.0075 +- 0.0044), since the arm's options start from different places. The flips
are not noise: the surf changes its mind for reasons that pay more often than not. The switch is removed in the same PR.

## Where this leaves the 1v1 movement question

- Not the mix of views, not commitment. The first rating's edge over chance is 8 points; the re-ratings add 1.4 more.
- The hits the surf foresees and still takes (54% of hits in the chosen first option) are the largest class: at that
  moment every option it has is rated close, and the one it takes gets hit. More options, or options further apart
  (a wider stop, a reverse), is the untested geometry lever; the real-hit column of `aes_round.py` is its gate.

## Option geometry: half-speed orbits (MOVE-37, cloud arm sl, removed)

Hypothesis: when the three options are rated close, the surf has nowhere to go between a stop and full speed; two
half-speed orbit options (one each way, speed 4) give it intermediate destinations. Run `work/slow-forensic` (same 32
learning guns, seeds 10901-10902, c = aa,aesrec vs sl = slow,aesrec; table in
`docs/bench/local/2026-11-09_slow-forensic-aes.md`; the box restarted five times during the run, the bench resumed each time).

- The surf took a half-speed option on 15.6% of first ratings (7.8% each way); stop fell from 16% to 11%.
- Real hit share c 0.086, sl 0.082 pooled: lower in rounds 7-34 (0.087 -> 0.082) but higher in rounds 0-6; paired per
  opponent -0.0023 +- 0.0028 real hits per wave, fewer hits on 17 of 28 guns. Real damage per wave rose (0.482 -> 0.499):
  the hits it still takes are heavier.
- Battle scores over the 63 paired battles: score share -0.0087 +- 0.0138, survival share -0.0143 +- 0.0163, bullet damage
  taken equal (+0.9 +- 35.9), bullet damage dealt -17 +- 42, skipped turns +1.9 +- 1.4 per battle (five options cost CPU).

Verdict: not a lever as built. The hit reduction is inside its interval, the damage taken is unchanged and the score
goes the other way. The switch is reverted in the same PR; the AES record and readers keep taking any option count.

## A member that models the gun's recent aims (MOVE-33 mimics for every opponent, cloud arm mm)

The shipped code already carries such a member: the six learning-gun mimics (MOVE-33, `MIMIC_MEMBERS`, on
per opponent only through the mode table's `mimic-on` lines). The forensic turns them on for every opponent
(`work/mimic-forensic`, same 32 guns, seeds 11101-11102, arms c = aa,aesrec and mm = mimmem,aesrec,
`docs/bench/local/2026-11-10_mimic-forensic-aes.md`; the box restarted five times and the run resumed each time):

- real hit share c 0.085, mm 0.088 pooled; equal in round 0 (0.091 both) and worse with the mimics in every
  later round bucket (1-2: 0.087 vs 0.089; 3-6: 0.084 vs 0.088; 7-14: 0.083 vs 0.087; 15-34: 0.085 vs 0.088)
- paired per opponent +0.0018 +- 0.0031 real hits per wave (more hits), fewer on 12 of 28; against the learning
  guns the members are meant to read, worse: ScalarR +0.006, Shadow +0.008, Glacier +0.010, Wodan +0.013
- the mimics do not order the options better either: blend ranks the hit option top 0.432 vs 0.431
- paired battle score share mm - c -0.77 +- 1.15 (64 pairs), mm better in 27 of 64; mean 78.46 vs 79.23

Verdict: nothing. A model of the gun's last aims, fed from the shooter's side, reads the bullet no better than
the histogram views from round 1 on and costs score. No code changes: the switch ships as it is, per opponent.
